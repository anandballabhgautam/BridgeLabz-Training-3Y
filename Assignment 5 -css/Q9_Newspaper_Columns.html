The Daily Chronicle: City Launches Green Initiative

The city council announced a new plan on Monday to plant ten thousand trees across public parks, roadsides and school grounds over the next two years. Officials say the project will reduce heat, improve air quality and create shaded places for families to spend time outdoors.

Volunteers from local colleges and community groups will help with the planting drive. Training sessions will be held every weekend, where residents can learn how to choose the right saplings, prepare the soil and care for young trees during the first difficult months.

Environmental experts welcomed the decision but warned that planting is only the first step. Trees need regular watering and protection, and without a proper maintenance plan many saplings may not survive the hot summer season that follows every spring.

Local shopkeepers also expressed support. Many believe that greener streets will attract more visitors to the market area and make the neighbourhood a more pleasant place to walk, shop and meet friends in the evening hours.

The council has set aside a dedicated budget for the project and promised to publish progress reports every three months. Residents can follow the updates on notice boards and through the city's information desk, and can suggest locations for new plantations.

School principals have already invited the project team to hold awareness sessions for students. Teachers say that involving children early helps build lasting habits of respecting nature and keeping the surroundings clean for everyone.

If the first phase succeeds, officials plan to extend the programme to nearby villages and to introduce rainwater harvesting at community buildings. The mayor described the initiative as a long-term investment in the health of future generations.
